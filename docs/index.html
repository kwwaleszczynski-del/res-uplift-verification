Did the upgrade work?

Vortex generators, pitch and yaw software, blade repairs, panel washing or a new inverter promise +1 to +3 % more energy, while the weather moves output by far more than that. This page compares five causal methods for measuring such effects from SCADA data against a group of untouched control units, validated by injecting known effects into real data from an open wind farm (Kelmarsh, 6 turbines, 2016-2021) and an open PV site (NREL PVDAQ, four roof systems in Las Vegas, 2016-2018).

Method comparison on injected effects

Each trial takes a real test unit at a random date, a random set of controls and a known injected effect; every method then estimates the effect over a post-period window. A good method has low error, intervals that contain the truth as often as they claim (dashed line), and rarely reports a "success" (lower 90 % bound above zero) when nothing was done (dashed line at 5 %).

Test-plan calculator

How long after an intervention must data be collected to show an expected energy uplift? For a uniform effect every method here is scale-equivariant, so the chance that the lower 90 % bound ends above zero for any effect follows exactly from the zero-effect trials. Only windows in which the method stayed honest count: false-success rate at zero effect at most 10 % and 90 % coverage at least 80 %. For a partial-load upgrade (vortex generators), enter the expected energy gain, which is smaller than the gain in partial load.

Weekly ratio scatter: standard deviation of the weekly log ratio of test to control energy in the year before the intervention (in %). It scales the error spread and interval width linearly; leave the reference value if unknown. Results are extrapolated from two sites and a limited number of trials - treat them as an order of magnitude for planning, not as a guarantee.

Wake spillover: when a control stands in the test turbine's wake

An upgrade that makes the test turbine extract more energy also slows the wind behind it. A control in that wake then loses power, the counterfactual drops and the effect is overestimated. Here the injected partial-load uplift raises the test turbine's thrust by the same fraction and a Gaussian wake model (Bastankhah & Porte-Agel 2014) lowers the power of downstream controls. Removing the IEC 61400-12-1 disturbed sectors (directions with a control in the test turbine's wake) removes the bias by construction, at the cost of data: the right chart shows how much wider the intervals get.

Case studies on real events

Long service stops from the Kelmarsh status logs and multi-week inverter outages in the PVDAQ data. None of them was meant to raise output; the question is whether the unit came back as good as before. Methods and sources

Test-control bins: industry practice for side-by-side upgrade tests - the pre-period ratio of test to control energy in bins of the controls' wind speed and direction (PV: irradiance and sun azimuth) predicts the post-period test energy (Hwangbo et al. 2017; Ding 2019, ch. 7; binning after IEC 61400-12-1); moving-block bootstrap over days. Synthetic control: non-negative weights over the controls' daily energy (Abadie, Diamond & Hainmueller 2010) with the conformal inference of Chernozhukov, Wuethrich & Zhu (2021): null-imposed refits and moving-block permutations. BSTS: local level plus spike-and-slab regression on the controls and weather, Gibbs sampler, the CausalImpact approach (Brodersen et al. 2015). GBM counterfactual: gradient boosted trees on the controls and weather, blocked cross-conformal intervals for window totals (after Barber et al. 2021; Xu & Xie 2021). Difference-in-differences: log energy ratio before and after, moving-block bootstrap. Naive: the test unit against its own pre-period power curve (no controls).

Data: Kelmarsh wind farm data, C. Plumley (2022), Zenodo, CC BY 4.0. NREL PVDAQ public datasets, Deline et al. (2021), OEDI doi:10.25984/1846021, CC BY 4.0. Assumptions behind every method: no spillover of the intervention onto the controls and a stable relation between test and controls - both are tested above, and both fail sometimes in real data.
